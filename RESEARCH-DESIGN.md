**Implementation choices and their limits**

Afterlight combines generated paper scenery and sprite atlases with runtime gait, anticipation, follow-through, recovery and impact effects. Its eight travelers use 72 cached poses, continuous foot-anchored motion, 85 ms ordinary blends and 28 ms attack-impact blends. Cached crease lighting and inward cut rims preserve the original silhouette and ink. Four left-facing title travelers have distinct menu celebrations; 704 character-specific lines react to public board events. These are Afterlight’s authored presentation choices, not Nintendo animation specifications. [Animation](src/paper-animation.js), [title cast](src/title-cast.js), [dialogue](src/character-banter.js).

A Three.js diorama supplies deeper printed relief, closed folded wings and alpha-contour paper extrusions. Generated environmental pieces carry crease faces and support tabs; four illustrated foreground pieces add a near layer around the playfield. The accordion pleats and foreground are recorded in the same material pass as the gameplay art, with foreground drawn before the pass flushes and HUD text drawn afterward. These layers therefore share the scene's lighting profile. Deterministic two-dimensional simulation still determines collisions and results. Normal relief, ambient occlusion, roughness and selective metalness are Afterlight’s rendering choices. These references establish no claim about Nintendo’s skeletons, frame counts, interpolation, shaders or production tools. [World](src/paper-world.js), [environment](src/paper-environment.js), [sprite recording order](src/art.js), [sprite materials](src/sprite-materials.js).

The visual consistency pass retains the eight original character designs and existing generated action/motion atlases. Cached preparation measures each pose’s paper tone against the traveler’s idle reference, dyes a protected clothing region in one of eight muted outfit colors, and preserves alpha silhouettes and dark printed ink. This is source-art harmonization, not a claim that every character asset was regenerated. The same prepared portraits and poses are used throughout the title, selection, lobby, board and games. New generated replacements supply the twelve-piece general prop atlas and nine-piece folded-object atlas; derived surface maps follow the current printed sources. [Character pigment](src/paper-pigment.js), [asset inventory](assets-manifest.json), [sprite preparation](src/art.js).

Prop preparation recovers the connected artwork owned by each atlas cell while retaining its nominal placement and excluding adjacent pieces. Briar's follow-through uses a source rectangle extended 32 pixels to include an existing branch beyond that pose's grid cell; the general recovery margin remains 48 pixels. Neither process can restore pixels absent from the original sheet. The legacy `pixel-fx` key now loads newly generated `folded-paper-fx.webp` art, with clean paper chips, impact folds, dust and shield effects. These are source and material treatments, not additional gameplay geometry. [Atlas ownership](src/paper-atlas.js), [pose rectangles](src/paper-animation.js), [current asset inventory](assets-manifest.json).

A large eight-character picker makes the selected special visible beside the portraits. Lobby cutouts are larger, and a player can change only their own name/traveler before the session starts. Alegreya body text and Alegreya SC titles/HUD replace the previous condensed sans and monospace families; the portable builder embeds the font subsets. The gameplay backing canvas scales up to 1920×1080 with antialiased source art while retaining logical 960×540 gameplay coordinates. A subdued matte of the current scene fills surrounding minigame viewport bands without cropping the gameplay canvas or HUD. These are authored presentation changes; they are not evidence about Nintendo’s typography or raster-resolution pipeline. [Interface](src/main.js), [styles](src/style.css), [HUD font helper](src/game-hud.js), [official Alegreya catalog](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Alegreya), [official Alegreya SC catalog](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Alegreya+SC).

The design inference is that an action is easier to read when preparation, contact and recovery have distinct visual cues, and the score/objective stays visible. That is a design hypothesis rather than a measured usability result. Visual review, gameplay tests and the current browser evidence can find missing cues or clipping; they do not by themselves establish player comprehension or enjoyment.

Afterlight’s **custom UNO-inspired rules** use seven starting cards and a 48-pad circular board with animated symbols instead of pad numbers. Matching-number chains combine movement into one turn; same-symbol +2/+4 stacking lets a recipient pass debt onward or take it and finish the turn. Three card buffs add movement, points or a host-seeded turn-order shuffle. These are deliberately custom rules, not a statement of official UNO rules. [Card and party rules](src/board.js), [pad and buff definitions](src/board-mischief.js).

Highest final points wins. Emptying a hand triggers the ending and a rounded-up 25% bonus; normal completed laps award 25 points and minigame placements award 20/12/8/4. A final card applies its printed point/order effects, then closes the party and clears unresolved draw debt; it never forces another traveler to draw after closure. Nine stories offer eighteen choices, including queuing the next precision-extraction or shared-block challenge. The production registry contains **26 games**: thirteen platform-fighter objectives, Gullet Gala, Clockwork Surgery, Tottering Tower and ten other arcade games. [Current rules and catalog](README.md), [registry](src/minigames.js), [new challenges](src/boardgame-challenges.js).

Gullet Gala’s contested pellets, Bellows Boxing’s pressure-driven head pops, Surgery’s physical extraction channels and Tower’s changing support balance are original toy-like interactions. Their scores and controls belong to Afterlight. Shared food, alarms, blocks or enemies do not create a cooperative win condition: current placement rewards remain individual.

Surgery's sockets place original gear artwork around shadowed recesses. Its extraction routes use illustrated paper lips, inset shading, thin ink cuts and small printed creases that follow the physical route and pressure-dependent width. This replaces flat overlay shapes while retaining the existing collision and scoring model. A recessed appearance does not make the simulation three-dimensional. [Challenge rendering and rules](src/boardgame-challenges.js).

All 26 games receive seeded surprises with a 1.2-second warning. Each pool fits the native game: gusts and relic rain in moving arenas, train service changes, rhythm bonuses, maze recharge, surgery lulls and tower counterweights. The design inference is that a warning and an existing input response make a surprise more legible than an unexplained rule change. This remains a hypothesis to test with players. The host owns outcome state; visual scenery and warning rendering cannot award points or alter private cards. [Surprise rules](src/minigame-surprises.js), [recipient privacy](src/network.js).

Easy, Medium and Hard are original bot-control profiles, defaulting to Medium in production. Across all 26 games they vary reaction delay, decision cadence, missed actions, lapses, aim/timing error and noisy planning choices. Precision, grid and rhythm controls scale the baseline delays to their task. Hard still has bounded error; human rules, physics and points are unchanged. The public game state carries the selected profile and seeded bot-control state so prediction and host replay agree. This does not change the board’s card-selection policy or establish a measured human challenge curve. [Difficulty controls](src/bot-difficulty.js), [replay and human-trace checks](tests/bot-difficulty.test.js).

Lobby profile changes remain in the version-1 transport. A guest submits only their own name/character patch; the host validates the admitted connection, fields and limits, then broadcasts the authoritative roster. Edits are unavailable after lock/start. This UI convenience does not grant guest authority over cards, points or other travelers. [Profile API and roster validation](src/network.js), [network regressions](tests/network.test.js).

Heartbeat elapsed time uses a monotonic clock when available. Normal-cadence checks expire a connection after about 20 seconds of silence. A substantially delayed callback sends a probe and allows one traffic-processing grace window capped at 2.5 seconds; another late callback cannot extend that episode indefinitely. Received valid traffic resets the grace, while continued silence still expires it. This handles a specific delayed-timer mechanism; it does not establish the cause of an earlier disconnect or prove reliability across WAN/NAT configurations. [Heartbeat transport](src/network.js), [clock and bounded-grace checks](tests/network.test.js).

The animation and artwork build can change during development. Consult [the current verification record](VERIFICATION.md) for the exact exported artifact, tested scope and remaining limits; this note is research provenance, not a final release verification claim.

## Animation and physical paper follow-up

Nintendo's developer interview describes designing interactions around what happens to paper itself—folding, wetting, burning, attaching and peeling—and placing surprises into the map. Afterlight applies that material-first idea through bent-card throws, springy travelers, folded-door passage, paper encounter props and displaced rivals. The specific animations and rules here are original choices, not claims about Nintendo's implementation. [Nintendo, Iwata Asks: Brainstorming Paper Ideas](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-Paper-Mario-Sticker-Star/Paper-Mario-Sticker-Star/5-Brainstorming-Paper-Ideas/5-Brainstorming-Paper-Ideas-690707.html).

The public description of Masahiro Sakurai's *Attack Poses* episode emphasizes that attacks must read clearly; the video itself was not available through the research reader, so no internal pipeline details are attributed to it. Afterlight's interpolation, fixed feet anchors, generated anticipation/follow-through poses, short blends and spring parameters are its own implementation. [Masahiro Sakurai on Creating Games: Attack Poses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=99gdMDF7V2E).
