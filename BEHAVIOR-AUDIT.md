# Afterlight behavior audit

Source review: 2026-10-03. This compares the current implementation with selected, publicly documented behaviors from Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, Mario Party and Super Smash Bros. It is a design comparison, not a claim that Afterlight should reproduce every feature of those games. **Present** means implemented; **partial** means a related mechanic exists with less depth; **missing** means no implementation was found in the reviewed gameplay paths. These are source findings, not a new playtest or release certification.

The main remaining gaps are deeper recovery and defensive movement, paper transformations that change traversal, and party team/cooperative roles. The current registry contains **26 minigames**: thirteen platform-fighter objectives, Gullet Gala, Clockwork Surgery, Tottering Tower and ten other arcade games. Every game also has seeded, warned surprises. A larger catalog and more expressive motion establish implemented variety; they do not prove enjoyment. See [the active registry](src/minigames.js), [fighter objectives](src/brawl-objectives.js), [new challenges](src/boardgame-challenges.js) and [surprise rules](src/minigame-surprises.js).

## Paper Mario: appearance versus physical behavior

Nintendo describes TTYD's paper-plane and edge-on transformations, partners, theatrical turn-based battles, timed attacks and an audience that can help or hinder. These are documented game behaviors; the article does not establish Nintendo's internal animation or material-rendering techniques. [Official TTYD overview](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/join-mario-and-a-cast-of-oddballs-in-one-of-his-strangest-and-epic-est-rpg-adventures/)

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layered paper theater and expressive motion | **Present.** Deeper image-derived relief, four closed folded wings, alpha-contour extrusions with crease faces/support tabs, solid board pieces and four illustrated foreground pieces create scene depth. Characters use cached inward paper rims and folded-face lighting, preserving ink, alpha and foot anchors. The existing source poses are normalized to each traveler’s idle paper tone and clothing is dyed in eight distinct muted colors; alpha and dark ink remain protected. New general props/folded objects use clean outlined source art. Their 72 poses blend over 85 ms, or 28 ms for attack impacts, with continuous gait, landing and recoil. [World](src/paper-world.js), [environment](src/paper-environment.js), [pigment](src/paper-pigment.js), [animation](src/paper-animation.js). | Afterlight is a 2.5D theater: presentation has depth while authoritative movement and collisions remain two-dimensional. This establishes its own paper treatment, not parity with Nintendo's renderer. |
| Character reactions and theatrical framing | **Present as presentation.** The title's four travelers face left and celebrate menu choices. Board portraits keep the local traveler left and the active rival right; played cards travel to the ring center. The large picker exposes eight traveler portraits and a selected-special description; the lobby presents larger cutouts. Alegreya/Alegreya SC serve body, title and HUD text, with fonts embedded in the portable build. The gameplay canvas scales to a capped 1920×1080 backing store without changing logical coordinates. Eight travelers have 88 distinct contextual lines each, including good/bad landings, chains, stacks and rival replies. [Title cast](src/title-cast.js), [public presentation](src/board-presentation.js), [dialogue](src/character-banter.js), [picker and canvas](src/main.js), [font/style](src/style.css). | Dialogue is selected from public events. It does not reveal private cards or introduce an audience with gameplay powers. |
| Paper transformations change where the player can go | **Missing.** Gameplay uses fixed board connections or platform-top landing collision. No edge-on clearance, paper-plane flight, folding bridge or player-controlled unfolding collision state exists. [Board topology](src/board.js), [fighter landing collision](src/brawlers.js). | Ladders, chutes and moving platforms move players physically, but are not transformations of paper geometry. Folded rendering and animated paper effects do not establish transformation gameplay. |
| Partners, exploration, RPG battles and reactive audience | **Missing as a system.** Travelers have distinct combat specials, while the party structure is card turns and short competitive rounds. [Specials](src/brawlers.js), [party state and turns](src/board.js). | No exploration campaign, persistent partner abilities, quest progression or battle audience with gameplay consequences. These would change the project's genre. |

The current presentation uses newly generated folded-paper effects through the retained `pixel-fx` runtime key. Prop/object atlas preparation recovers connected artwork belonging to each original cell, excludes neighboring pieces and draws expanded crops at their nominal placement. Environmental pleats and illustrated foreground enter the shared scene-lighting material pass before its flush; HUD text follows afterward. Taller minigame viewports use a subdued matte of the current scene around the full gameplay canvas. These mechanisms address source clipping, lighting and framing; their presence does not establish visual cohesion without review of the actual rendered scenes. [Asset inventory](assets-manifest.json), [atlas ownership](src/paper-atlas.js), [recording order](src/art.js), [viewport framing](src/style.css).

## Mario Party: social and board depth

The official Jamboree overview describes four-player board turns, obstacles/items, minigame rewards, coin-to-Star decisions, boards with distinctive hazards, temporary buddies and a separate cooperative squad mode. [Official Jamboree overview](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/super-mario-party-jamboree-heres-a-quick-overview-of-the-game/) For an explicit series example of two-versus-two board planning and allies, Nintendo's **2018 Super Mario Party** page documents Partner Party; this is not being attributed to every Mario Party edition. [Official Partner Party description](https://www.nintendo.com/sg/switch/adfj/sugoroku/index.html)

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board turns lead into competitive minigames and rewards | **Present, with custom UNO-inspired rules.** Seven starting cards, 48 unnumbered animated-symbol pads, matching-number chains, same-symbol +2/+4 stack-or-take decisions and minigames after four completed turns. Normal laps award 25 points; clearing a hand closes the party with a rounded-up 25% bonus and clears unresolved debt. Highest final points wins; no rival draws after closure. [Party rules and projection](src/board.js). | Card movement and point victory deliberately replace dice and Stars. These are original house rules, not official UNO rules. Their difference is not itself an omitted implementation. |
| Rival interaction and meaningful landing choices | **Present, with bounded choices.** Nine stories offer eighteen choices; twelve ordinary pad kinds award points, tolls, protection, lanterns, boosts or private card buffs. High-fives pay both travelers; landings bump rivals unless shielded. Comet Fold adds movement, Ink Gleam adds points, and Clockwork Fold shuffles future turn order while preserving IDs, physical seats and host ownership. [Pad/buff definitions](src/board-mischief.js), [host resolution](src/board.js). | No shop or spendable board currency, negotiated trade, chosen duel, or companion whose allegiance changes. Point stealing and tolls exist, but points do not fund purchases. |
| Board-specific and changing geography | **Partial.** Nearby ladders/chutes, shortcuts and a folded-door teleport change the traveled route. Story reversal and card shuffles change turn order; clinic/workshop choices can queue the next challenge. [Connections and story outcomes](src/board.js). | One fixed board topology; no alternate boards, tide-like route availability or board-wide phase that opens/closes connections. Turn-order changes and minigame surprises do not change board geography. |
| Announced disruptions within a minigame | **Present, with original rules.** All 26 games use a separate seeded surprise stream and a 1.2-second warning. Effects fit native controls: arena gusts/relics, train service, rhythm bonuses, recharge changes, surgery lulls or tower counterweights. [Surprises](src/minigame-surprises.js), [game stepping](src/minigames.js). | These are bounded round events. Drawing warnings, scenery or particles cannot roll outcomes, grant points or expose private board state. Their effect on enjoyment needs human playtesting. |
| Team and cooperative roles | **Missing.** Scores, ranks and party rewards are individual. Colossus Wake and Hollow Horde share enemies; Surgery shares alarm pressure and Tower shares blocks/balance. Players still compete for individual scores. [Fighter objectives](src/brawl-objectives.js), [challenge rules](src/boardgame-challenges.js), [awards](src/board.js). | No two-versus-two or one-versus-three assignment, shared team resources or shared victory/failure condition. Shared targets alone do not establish cooperative play. |
| Adjustable computer rivals | **Present for minigames.** All 26 production modes accept Easy, Medium or Hard, defaulting to Medium. Profiles vary delayed input decisions, aim/timing error, missed actions, movement lapses and imperfect ranked choices; seeded public bot state survives JSON replay. The host controls online difficulty. [Profiles](src/bot-difficulty.js), [game creation](src/minigames.js), [replay and unchanged-human-input tests](tests/bot-difficulty.test.js). | The card-board bot policy is unchanged. No adaptive difficulty, individual per-bot levels or established human win-rate targets. Smaller error settings do not guarantee that every seed/game is harder for every player. |
| Personal lobby traveler choice | **Present.** A player opens their own large portrait picker, applies a name/character patch and receives the host’s authoritative roster. Version-1 messages validate fields, bounds and admitted connection ownership; lock/start closes edits. [UI](src/main.js), [profile API](src/network.js). | This is selection of one of eight authored characters, not custom outfits, independent loadouts, accounts or mid-match character switching. It adds no authority over another player or private cards. |

A related product limitation is local input: the local roster is one human plus three bots, and keyboard/touch input controls one local traveler. Multiple browsers can play online, but same-device multi-controller play and controller remapping are absent from the current input path. [Roster and input](src/main.js). The layout targets desktop; this is separate from minigame mechanics.

## Smash: combat fundamentals versus combat depth

Ultimate's official pages explain damage-dependent launch distance, returning after being launched, directional air dodges, short-hop attacks and timed shielding. These establish broad reference behaviors, not exact frame data for Afterlight. [Basics](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/basic.html), [advanced techniques](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/technique.html).

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform-fighter foundation | **Present.** Damage-scaled knockback, double jumps, aerial attacks, shields, a 0.13-second parry window, ring-outs, moving platforms and eight distinct specials. Launch/KO bodies visibly grow toward the camera or recede, with directional paper-chip bursts and elastic landings. [Simulation and reactions](src/brawlers.js), [objectives](src/brawl-objectives.js). | Visual scale changes preserve authoritative hitboxes. Objective differences change what fighting accomplishes; presentation alone does not deepen recovery decisions. |
| Recovery and defensive movement | **Partial.** Air steering, a second jump, guarding and special movement exist. [Movement and landing](src/brawlers.js). | No directional air dodge, ground roll/spot dodge, ledge grab/get-up choice, platform drop-through or variable-height/short-hop input. Landing checks downward crossing of platform tops. These verified absences leave fewer available recovery/defense actions; their practical effect requires playtesting. |
| Character and move-set depth | **Partial.** Four contextual ordinary attacks are shared; each traveler adds one distinct special with real windup, active and recovery phases. [Moves and specials](src/brawlers.js). | No grab/throw interaction, full directional special set, or distinct basic movement/weight model per traveler. Ordinary attacks outside Bellows Boxing do not have held-release charging; Boxing punches and Gullet gulps do. |
| Timing matches the visible action | **Partial; a current source concern.** Ordinary attacks become collision-active immediately, while the animator selects approximately **30 ms of anticipation** before its attack peak. Impact pauses freeze the pose, while simulation movement and attack clocks continue. [Attack activation and hit condition](src/brawlers.js), [visual anticipation and freeze](src/paper-animation.js). | An ordinary strike can connect during its preparatory pose. This identifies a possible feedback mismatch, not a measured cause of poor feel. Specials and Boxing punches have actual windups; the concern does not apply to every attack. Longer pose blends smooth rendering but do not change hit windows. |

Wave and distance objectives are already represented by Hollow Horde and Relic Launch. Calling them missing would be incorrect. They are original adaptations of broad objective types also described on the [official Smash modes page](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/mode.html), not reproductions of those modes' full rules.

Current combat guards prevent a stunned actor from starting or dealing an ordinary strike, resolve fresh simultaneous swings without granting the first seat exclusive priority, and end a solo stock round when its traveler reaches zero stocks. These cases have focused regression tests; they should not remain listed as open bugs. [Combat](src/brawlers.js), [regressions](tests/brawlers-paper-reactions.test.js).

Gullet Gala implements contested feeding: swept bites claim individual moving pellets once, charged gulps trade reach for cooldown, hazards delay mouths, and burps push pellets or interrupt rivals. It uses a two-dimensional mouth/neck simulation rather than articulated rigid-body jaws. [Feeding rules](src/gullet-gala.js). Bellows Boxing has real punch windups, guard/parry and pressure-triggered head pops; it does not have separately controlled fists or a duck/weave subsystem. [Boxing rules](src/brawl-objectives.js).

Clockwork Surgery implements carried organs, swept channel-contact checks, clean chains, rotating bounties, shared alarm pressure, restocking patients and a timed steady-hand action. Tottering Tower uses exclusive block claims, gradual pulling, mass/support clearance, counterbalance, off-center stacking, a finite brace reserve and attributed collapse penalties. These are physical, stateful challenges rather than score-only overlays. They still use deterministic two-dimensional models, not full rigid-body simulation. [Challenge source](src/boardgame-challenges.js), [focused challenge tests](tests/boardgame-challenges.test.js).

Surgery's rendering now uses original gear rims around inset-shadow organ sockets, with illustrated paper lips and printed crease marks along the extraction recesses. The recess width follows the existing pressure-dependent channel geometry. This changes the pictured material and depth without changing collision routes, extraction controls or scoring. [Socket/channel rendering](src/boardgame-challenges.js).

The character consistency pass reuses the existing original designs/action sheets, measures paper tone and applies protected clothing dye during cache preparation. It does not regenerate every character image. Pip, Moth, Bolt, Wisp, Rook, Briar, Vellum and Nix have separate outfit colors that remain consistent between portrait and gameplay preparation. Clean antialiased sampling, higher-resolution caches and derived surface maps improve the rendering source; this does not establish animation quality or enjoyment without visual/human review. [Tone and cloth masks](src/paper-pigment.js), [source preparation](src/art.js), [asset inventory](assets-manifest.json).

Briar's follow-through source rectangle includes 32 additional pixels on the right to recover a branch already present beyond its nominal atlas cell. The general connected-source crop margin remains 48 pixels. This is a specific source-cut correction, not a regenerated pose or a claim that missing artwork outside the sheet can be restored. [Motion source rectangles](src/paper-animation.js).

The host validates cards, chains, penalty responses, story choices, points and results. Clients predict responsive six-button minigame controls. Personalized snapshots expose only the recipient's card faces/buffs; played cards, debt totals, buff-gift kind/count and shuffled turn-order IDs are public. Host-private decks and random state are removed. Neither prediction nor the 2.5D renderer creates a trusted competitive server. [Recipient views](src/board.js), [transport](src/network.js), [prediction](src/realtime.js).

The heartbeat clock is monotonic when available. Normal timer cadence expires approximately 20 seconds of silence; an unusually late callback sends a probe and grants one traffic-processing interval capped at 2.5 seconds. Continued silence expires that bounded interval even if the next callback is also late, while received valid traffic clears it. Focused source checks cover clock choice and both host/guest grace paths. This identifies the current transport behavior, without attributing any earlier browser disconnect to timer lateness or establishing WAN/NAT coverage. Host departure still ends the room; there is no host migration. [Heartbeat implementation](src/network.js), [transport tests](tests/network.test.js).

## Priorities — design judgment, not verified outcomes

1. **Check ordinary visual contact against hit timing.** The verified immediate-hit/30 ms preparation relationship identifies a possible feedback mismatch. Aligning the initial visual strike with immediate input, or deliberately defining startup, requires focused playtesting. Changing simulation pause also affects responsiveness and prediction; it is a separate decision.
2. **Add one recovery/defense decision.** A ledge recovery or air dodge, plus deliberate platform drop-through, would create options in states that currently have few. Preserve simple controls; a full Smash move set is not required.
3. **Add a genuinely cooperative or team round.** Shared success, complementary jobs or team resources would change the social decisions more than another individually scored target game.
4. **Make one paper transformation physical.** A folding bridge, edge-on passage or gliding segment whose collision/traversal actually changes would connect the material style to player decisions.
5. **Evaluate the new card and pad decisions before adding another economy.** Chains, debt stacks, three buffs, twelve pad effects and nine stories already expand planning. Spendable rewards or temporary allies could add choices but also complicate pacing and explanation. Full RPG progression is a larger departure.

The reasoning is: verified limitations identify fewer available actions; additional actions could create more interesting decisions; whether they do so depends on clarity, balance and human play. Automated test coverage, asset count and frame-rate measurements do not prove that last step. Reducing screen text should preserve warnings and feedback needed to understand the mechanics already present, rather than using more permanent labels to compensate for unclear behavior.
