# Afterlight behavior audit

Source review: 2026-10-03. This compares the current implementation with selected, publicly documented behaviors from Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door, Mario Party and Super Smash Bros. It is a design comparison, not a claim that Afterlight should reproduce every feature of those games. **Present** means implemented; **partial** means a related mechanic exists with less depth; **missing** means no implementation was found in the reviewed gameplay paths. These are source findings, not a new playtest or release certification.

The main gaps are deeper recovery and defensive movement, paper transformations that change traversal, and party team/cooperative roles. They are not a shortage of game modes: the current registry contains 24 minigames, including 13 platform-fighter objectives and Gullet Gala. See [the active registry](src/minigames.js#L6), [fighter registry](src/brawlers.js#L4) and [objective definitions](src/brawl-objectives.js#L2).

## Paper Mario: appearance versus physical behavior

Nintendo describes TTYD's paper-plane and edge-on transformations, partners, theatrical turn-based battles, timed attacks and an audience that can help or hinder. These are documented game behaviors; the article does not establish Nintendo's internal animation or material-rendering techniques. [Official TTYD overview](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/join-mario-and-a-cast-of-oddballs-in-one-of-his-strangest-and-epic-est-rpg-adventures/)

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layered paper theater and expressive motion | **Present.** Image-derived relief, closed folded scenery wings, solid paper board pieces, paper material maps and scene lights coexist with sprite anticipation, attack peaks, follow-through, recovery and gait transitions. [World renderer](src/paper-world.js), [board solids](src/paper-board.js), [geometry](src/paper-geometry.js), [pose selection](src/paper-animation.js#L69). | This establishes Afterlight's own paper presentation, not parity with Nintendo's renderer. |
| Paper transformations change where the player can go | **Missing.** The scenery renderer takes scene ID, time and lighting; gameplay uses fixed board connections or ordinary platform collision. No edge-on clearance, paper-plane flight, folding bridge or player-controlled unfolding collision state exists. [Scenery draw contract](src/paper-world.js), [board topology](src/board.js#L6), [fighter landing collision](src/brawlers.js#L171). | The paper material largely affects appearance. Ladders, chutes and moving platforms do move players physically, but are not transformations of paper geometry. |
| Partners, exploration, RPG battles and reactive audience | **Missing as a system.** Travelers have distinct combat specials, while the party structure is card turns and short competitive rounds. [Specials](src/brawlers.js#L17), [party state](src/board.js#L177), [turn transition](src/board.js#L244). | No exploration campaign, persistent partner abilities, quest progression or battle audience with gameplay consequences. These would change the project's genre and are lower priority than improving its party loop. |

## Mario Party: social and board depth

The official Jamboree overview describes four-player board turns, obstacles/items, minigame rewards, coin-to-Star decisions, boards with distinctive hazards, temporary buddies and a separate cooperative squad mode. [Official Jamboree overview](https://www.nintendo.com/en-ca/whatsnew/super-mario-party-jamboree-heres-a-quick-overview-of-the-game/) For an explicit series example of two-versus-two board planning and allies, Nintendo's **2018 Super Mario Party** page documents Partner Party; this is not being attributed to every Mario Party edition. [Official Partner Party description](https://www.nintendo.com/sg/switch/adfj/sugoroku/index.html)

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board turns lead into competitive minigames and rewards | **Present, with original rules.** Seven starting cards, 48 spaces, route choices, minigames after four completed turns, ranked points, lap rewards and a proportional finishing bonus. Clearing a hand ends the match; highest final points wins. [Party creation](src/board.js#L177), [turns](src/board.js#L244), [finish projection](src/board.js#L102), [minigame awards](src/board.js#L581). | Card movement and point victory deliberately replace dice and Stars. Their difference is not itself a defect or an omitted implementation. |
| Rival interaction and meaningful landing choices | **Present, but narrower.** Seven story events offer fourteen choices; high-fives pay both travelers, landings bump rivals, shields absorb bumps, and boosts affect movement. [Events](src/board.js#L86), [event resolution](src/board.js#L296), [encounters](src/board.js#L350). | No shop or spendable board currency, negotiated trade, chosen duel, or companion whose allegiance changes. The pocket holds only boost/shield items. [Authoritative action set](src/board.js#L449). Points mostly accumulate rather than fund strategic purchases. |
| Board-specific and changing geography | **Partial.** Nearby ladders/chutes, shortcuts, a folded-door teleport and reversed turn order create real traversal changes. [Connections](src/board.js#L6), [story outcomes](src/board.js#L296). | One fixed board topology; no alternate boards, tide-like route availability or board-wide phase that opens/closes connections. A teleport is not a changing map. |
| Team and cooperative roles | **Missing.** Scores, ranks and party rewards are individual. Colossus Wake and Hollow Horde have shared enemies, but individual damage/knockout competition rather than a team victory or assigned cooperative roles. [Objective initialization](src/brawl-objectives.js#L29), [objective rules](src/brawl-objectives.js#L91), [individual awards](src/board.js#L581). | No two-versus-two or one-versus-three round assignment, shared team resources or shared success/failure condition. Shared targets alone do not establish cooperative play. |

A related product limitation is local input: the local roster is one human plus three bots, and keyboard/touch input controls one local traveler. Multiple browsers can play online, but same-device multi-controller play and controller remapping are absent from the current input path. [Local roster](src/main.js#L74), [keyboard mapping](src/main.js#L153). This is separate from minigame mechanics.

## Smash: combat fundamentals versus combat depth

Ultimate's official pages explain damage-dependent launch distance, returning after being launched, directional air dodges, short-hop attacks and timed shielding. [Basics](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/basic.html), [advanced techniques](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/technique.html) Nintendo's older **Smash for Wii U** manual supplies explicit series examples of ledge options, platform drop-through, charged smash attacks, grabs and throws; it is not evidence for exact Ultimate frame data. [Official manual, sections 9–11](https://www.nintendo.com/eu/media/downloads/games_8/emanuals/wii_u_6/super_smash_bros__for_wii_u/ElectronicManual_WiiU_SuperSmashBrosForWiiU_EN.pdf)

| Behavior | Current status and evidence | What remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform-fighter foundation | **Present.** Damage-scaled knockback, double jumps, aerial attacks, shields, a 0.13-second parry window, ring-outs, moving platforms and eight mechanically distinct specials. [Strike resolution](src/brawlers.js#L177), [movement/defense](src/brawlers.js#L234), [special state machine](src/brawlers.js#L43). | These are genuine mechanics, not cosmetic attack animations. Different objectives already change what fighting accomplishes. |
| Recovery and defensive movement | **Partial.** Air steering, a second jump, guarding and special movement exist. [Movement](src/brawlers.js#L239). | No directional air dodge, ground roll/spot dodge, ledge grab/get-up choice, platform drop-through or variable-height/short-hop input. Landing checks only downward crossing of platform tops. [Landing](src/brawlers.js#L171). This leaves fewer responses when displaced or pressured near an edge. |
| Character and move-set depth | **Partial.** Four contextual ordinary attacks are shared; each traveler adds one distinct special with real windup, active and recovery phases. [Shared moves](src/brawlers.js#L16), [special definitions](src/brawlers.js#L17). | No grab/throw interaction, ordinary held-release charged strike, full directional special set, or distinct basic movement/weight model per traveler. Do not confuse this with no charging anywhere: Gullet has charged gulps. |
| Timing matches the visible action | **Partial; a concrete review concern.** Ordinary attacks become collision-active immediately, while the animator first selects roughly 50 ms of anticipation. Impact pauses freeze the pose, but the simulation still advances movement and attack clocks. [Attack activation](src/brawlers.js#L244), [active-hit condition](src/brawlers.js#L27), [visual anticipation](src/paper-animation.js#L69), [visual freeze](src/paper-animation.js#L54), [simulation step](src/brawlers.js#L221). | An ordinary strike can connect during its preparatory pose. This is a source-level readability risk, not proof of a measured cause of poor feel. Specials and Boxing punches already have actual windups, so the issue must not be generalized to every attack. [Boxing exception](src/brawlers.js#L246). |

Wave and distance objectives are already represented by Hollow Horde and Relic Launch. Calling them missing would be incorrect. They are original adaptations of broad objective types also described on the [official Smash modes page](https://www.smashbros.com/en_US/howtoplay/mode.html), not reproductions of those modes' full rules.

Gullet Gala also implements the core contested-feeding interaction: swept bites claim individual moving pellets once, charged gulps trade reach for cooldown, hazards delay mouths, and burps push pellets or interrupt rivals. Its simplification is a two-dimensional mouth/neck simulation rather than articulated rigid-body jaws. [Charge/burp behavior](src/gullet-gala.js#L32), [swept claims](src/gullet-gala.js#L87). Bellows Boxing has real punch windups, guard/parry and pressure-triggered head pops; it does not have separately controlled fists or a duck/weave subsystem. [Boxing hit rules](src/brawl-objectives.js#L65), [round behavior](src/brawl-objectives.js#L127).

## Priorities — design judgment, not verified outcomes

1. **Align ordinary hit timing with the visible strike.** The verified activation/anticipation mismatch gives a specific mechanism for ambiguous feedback. Matching collision startup and visual contact, and deciding whether impact pause should stop simulation, is a smaller change than adding more modes. A focused playtest must establish whether it improves readability.
2. **Add one recovery/defense decision.** A ledge recovery or air dodge, plus deliberate platform drop-through, would create options in states that currently have few. Preserve simple controls; a full Smash move set is not required.
3. **Add a genuinely cooperative or team round.** Shared success, complementary jobs or team resources would change the social decisions more than another individually scored target game.
4. **Make one paper transformation physical.** A folding bridge, edge-on passage or gliding segment whose collision/traversal actually changes would connect the material style to player decisions.
5. **Consider spendable rewards or temporary allies later.** These could deepen board planning but also complicate pacing and explanation. Full RPG progression is a much larger departure.

The reasoning is: verified limitations identify fewer available actions; additional actions could create more interesting decisions; whether they do so depends on clarity, balance and human play. Automated test coverage, asset count and frame-rate measurements do not prove that last step. Reducing screen text should preserve warnings and feedback needed to understand the mechanics already present, rather than using more permanent labels to compensate for unclear behavior.
